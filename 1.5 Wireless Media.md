2.4 GHz vs 5 GHz vs 6 GHz  
- Wireless frequencies transmit data using radio waves. All waves have a pattern and can be described by a wavelength (the distance a wave travels before the pattern repeats itself) and a frequency (how many times the pattern repeats over a given time period).  
- These frequencies act like pipes transporting water. As the pipes shrink in size (2.4 GHz), less water is let through the pipe. However, we can transport the water farther distances. As the pipe widens in size (5/6 GHz), more water is let through the pipe. However, we can't transport this intensity of water as far. In total, 2.4 GHz can carry data farther and has less issues with physical obstructions, but carries less data in total. 5 GHz and 6 GHz can carry far more data. However, this data can only be transmitted in shorter distances and can be significantly impacted by physical obstructions.
- However, although this pipe metaphor works great for explaining how frequency rates affect throughput and range. It only works in a bubble. Realistically, any wireless network will have many devices on the same network within the same frequency range. In the case for the 2.4 GHz range, because the "pipe" is smaller, it takes fewer devices within the same frequency to clog the "pipe" causing more interference. Other devices may also use the 2.4 GHz range such as conventional microwaves, other IOT devices, and bluetooth connected devices in some cases. Causing 2.4 GHz to be affected by what is called EMI (Electromagnetic Interference) 

802.11A  
Operates in the 5 GHz band  
Max rate of 54 Mbps

802.11B  
Operates in the 2.4 GHz band  
Max rate of 11 Mb  
Created to improve range and obstacle penetration  
More susceptible to interference

802.11G  
Operates in the 2.4 GHz band  
Max rate of 54 Mb  
Created to improve maximum throughput of B while keeping the extended range  
Backward compatible with 802.11B devices

802.11N  
Operates in the 2.4 AND 5 GHz ranges  
Increases maximum data rate to 600 Mb by utilizing multiple antennas/MIMO technology (Multiple-Input Multiple-Output)

802.11AC  
Operates in the 5 GHz band  
Max data rate of 3.5 Gb by using wider channels, more spacial streams, and higher modulation  
Introduces MU-MIMO (Multi-User MIMO), enhancing existing MIMO technology

802.11AX  
Operates in the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands (6 GHz using WiFi 6e)  
Max rate of 9.6 Gb  
Further WLAN efficiency, especially in crowded spaces by offering higher data rates, better coverage, and reduced power consumption by introducing OFDMA and BSS coloring
